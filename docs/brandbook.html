Brand idea

"Holdline" is a working name. Change it in the heading and the title if the team picks another.

The product

A web simulator for money conversations: a raise, an offer, rent, a bill, a card rate. The user plays the conversation against a counterpart who does not give in for free, then sees how much money was left on the table and on which move.

The picture

Every negotiation is two numbers and a gap between them: what the other side could pay, and where you stopped. The identity is built from that picture. Horizontal bars, one big mono figure, one amber gap. Nothing decorative that does not carry a number.

Colour

A cool neutral ground, one brand hue, and two meaning colours. Dark is a full theme, not an inversion. Meaning colours always travel with a word or sign, and green and amber differ in lightness as well as hue. There is no red: a missed chance is not an error.

Shape and space

Flat fields, 1px line borders, radius 4/8/12, one hairline shadow. No gradients, no glass, no glow. Left-aligned on a 4px grid with a 16px side gutter on phones. The chat sits in one column of at most 720px; the debrief widens to two columns above 900px.

Imagery and icons

Counterpart avatars are flat illustrations in one style, in hold-tint, gap-tint and ink. No generated photoreal faces. Icons are Lucide at 1.5px stroke, 20px, in ink or muted. No emoji as icons.

Voice

Direct, calm, a little dry. A coach who has seen this conversation fifty times and does not flatter.

  • Talk to the user as "you". Name the counterpart by role ("your manager"), never "the AI".
  • Lead with the number. "You stopped at $104,000. The ceiling was $112,000."
  • Say what a move did. Name the move type, then the fix.
  • Buttons are verbs: "Start", "Replay this round", "See the debrief".
  • No exclamation marks, no emoji, no "unlock", no "supercharge", no fear copy.
  • Be honest about the simulation. The ceiling is a scenario setting from the range the user entered. Say "against your range", never "your boss would have paid".

Product UI stack

  • Next.js + TypeScript for the app and API routes.
  • Tailwind CSS with these tokens mapped to the theme (colors.paper, colors.hold, fontFamily.display). Never retype hex.
  • shadcn/ui (Radix) for dialog, tabs, tooltip, progress and form controls, restyled with these tokens. Do not ship its default grey and zinc.
  • Lucide icons, 1.5px stroke, 20px.
  • CSS transitions for the meters and message entry. Framer Motion only if needed.
  • Sonner for toasts, if any.

Pages, in build order

  1. Quiz: one question per screen with a progress track.
  2. Session: chat plus the two meters.
  3. Debrief: offer bar, ledger, "Replay this round".
  4. Pricing: plain, no countdowns, trial and renewal prices shown before payment.

Usage rules

  1. Body text is ink on paper or raised. Secondary text is muted, never lighter.
  2. The primary button is hold with on-hold text. One per screen.
  3. The user's bubble is hold-tint with ink text. The counterpart's is raised with a line border.
  4. Money figures use the mono face. Gains are won with a plus, gaps are gap with the word "left".
  5. Focus ring: 2px hold outline, 2px offset, on every interactive element.
  6. Check contrast in both themes. Text is at least 4.5:1.

Avoid

  • A purple or blue gradient hero, or a glossy gradient button.
  • Inter or Space Grotesk as the "safe" face.
  • Emoji cards, and accent bars on the left edge of rounded cards.
  • A generated photoreal face for the counterpart.
  • Countdown timers, "only 3 spots" and other pressure patterns.
  • Copying Coursiv's or Zimran's marks, colours or mascot. Learn from the quiz, plan, paywall order, not the look.
